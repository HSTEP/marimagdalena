{# Twenty-one identical squares in a flat 3×7 grid is a contact sheet, not a collection — the deepest page on the site was also the flattest, and the homepage reel it is supposed to pay off gave each frame a title and a state while this gave them nothing. Set as plates instead, on a measure of seven over six columns: a row of three at index scale, then two pairs at reading scale, the second pair square again. Twenty-one divides by seven exactly, so the sequence closes on a complete row. Each plate used to carry its number under a hairline, defended as "the only caption these frames can honestly hold". The honest conclusion was the other one: they hold none. A plate number exists so that running text can say "see plate 14" — nothing on this site cites one, so twenty-one figures and twenty-one rules were a numbering scheme with no referent, which is the look of a table with the column headings taken off. The shoot is named once, above; the frames are the frames. #} {# Broken in two, at seven. Set as one run this was 5,799px of pictures — 78% of the page, about six and a half screens — between the attribution above it and the ceník note at the bottom, with not one word in it. Cutting the plate numbers was right; they were a numbering scheme with no referent. But twenty-one hairlines were also the only thing pacing the scroll, and taking them out without putting anything back left a folder of images where a catalogue had been. A printed catalogue of twenty-one plates has chapter breaks. It does not run six spreads without a running head. The break is at seven because the sequence already leans that way. Five of the seven process frames — the artistic team on the stairs, the briefing, the colour swatches, the whiteboard, the empty studio before anyone is in it — fall inside the first seven positions; only two of the remaining fourteen are process shots, and the rest is the finished thing, the styled looks and the boxes. That is the curator's own file ordering, not a division imposed on it, so the heads only name what is already there. Hence "Nejdřív" rather than a claim that the halves are pure. Seven is also exactly one cycle of the 7n layout rhythm and fourteen is exactly two, and `nth-child` counts within its own parent, so both halves open on a row of three and close on a complete row. Splitting anywhere else would have left a stub. #}
